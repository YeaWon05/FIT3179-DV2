Fishing and Recreational Angling in Australia

This visualisation explores recreational fishing across Australia. It examines participation, catch, locations, species, and fishing effort.

1. Recreational Fishing Participation Across Australia

The Northern Territory has the highest participation rate at 32.72%. Victoria has the lowest participation rate at 16.42%. Tasmania, Queensland, South Australia, and Western Australia have similar rates. NSW/ACT has a participation rate of 19.55%.


2. Recreational Fishing Catch by Region in Queensland

South-eastern catchment has the highest catch at 21.88 million. Central Coast catchment follows with 16.77 million. Torres Strait coastal waters has the lowest catch at 56,000. South-eastern and coastal regions generally show higher catches.


3. Recreational Fishing Locations in Victoria

Australian Salmon appears across many Victorian fishing locations. Snapper and Whiting are also commonly recorded. Several locations contain multiple fish species. Fishing locations span Victoria's coastlines, bays, rivers, and inlets.


4. Estimated Recreational Fish Catch by State and Species

NSW has the highest harvest of Flathead and Bream. Queensland has the highest Whiting and Mullet harvest. South Australia records the highest Garfish harvest. Queensland leads Whiting harvest, followed by Western Australia.


5. Estimated Catch by Species in NSW

Bream has the highest released catch at approximately 767. Nippers have the highest kept catch at approximately 669. Snapper releases greatly exceed the amount kept. Yellowtail Scad, nippers, prawns, and squid have higher kept catches.


6. Recreational Fishing Effort by Waterbody Type

Estuaries account for the largest share of fishing effort. Rivers and inshore waters also attract many recreational fishers. Offshore waters account for the smallest share of effort.


7. Changes in Recreational Fish Catch by Species

Bream consistently has the highest total catch across all years. Bream catch reached 1.27 million in 2023/24. Snapper catch fluctuated, reaching 517,486 in 2023/24. Australian Salmon catch remained relatively low throughout the period.


8. Recreational Fishing Effort by Survey Year

Fisher days declined from 2,391 in 2013–14 to 1,654 in 2019–20. Fisher days gradually increased, reaching 2,111 in 2023–24. The latest figure remains below the 2013–14 level.
